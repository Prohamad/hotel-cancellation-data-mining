Answers

RQ1classification

Can cancellation be predicted at booking time?

Yes, partly. The random forest classifies 80.7% of bookings correctly (AUC 0.837, F1 59.6%) and finds about half of the cancellations. Lead time is the strongest attribute: 9% of bookings made within 8 days are canceled, 33% of the rest. Non Refund deposits (94.7%), Online TA (35.5%) and Transient customers (30.3%) carry the highest risk.

RQ2numeric prediction

Does ADR vary by month and hotel type?

Yes. August is about €49 per night above April and January €31 below. The Resort Hotel ranges from €51 (January) to €189 (August); the City Hotel from €87 to €127. Decision tree regression explains 75.4% of ADR variance.

RQ3clustering

Can bookings be grouped into segments?

Yes, into four. Large, expensive bookings are canceled most (39%); single guests and guests with special requests least (19–20%). Silhouette 0.32 — the segments are only moderately separated.

Recommendations for hotels

Plan overbooking 01

Use the predicted cancellation probability per booking, not one average rate, to decide how many rooms to overbook.

Target early bookings 02

Bookings made months ahead through online agents are the riskiest — send reminders or ask for a deposit closer to arrival.

Encourage special requests 03

Guests who add requests cancel far less; asking about preferences after booking may increase commitment.

Price by season 04

Resort prices nearly quadruple in summer; cancellation is also higher in peak months, so peak bookings need the closest monitoring.

Limitations

Class imbalanceAt the default 0.5 threshold, all models detect at most about half of the canceled bookings.
ScopeTwo hotels in Portugal, 2015–2017 — results may not transfer to other hotels or to the period after 2020.
Missing contextNo price changes after booking, competitor prices or customer history beyond previous cancellations.
Warehouse rowsThe data warehouse holds 86,637 bookings (before removing the 17 outliers); the models use 86,620.
